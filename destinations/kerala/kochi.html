Kochi Travel Guide: Fort Kochi, Chinese Fishing Nets & Heritage

Historic cantilevered Chinese Fishing Nets silhouetted along the Fort Kochi waterfront at twilight, Kerala.

Best Time to Visit October to March

Airport Cochin International Airport (COK)
Rail Stations Ernakulam Junction (ERS) / Town (ERN)

Why Visit Kochi?

Kochi, historically celebrated as the Queen of the Arabian Sea, has welcomed international seafarers, spice traders, and explorers since ancient Roman times. Following the historic 1341 AD flood that carved open its natural harbor, Kochi became the premier trading post for black pepper and cardamom, drawing Arab, Chinese, Portuguese, Dutch, and British merchants.

Fort Kochi preserves an evocative maritime atmosphere: massive cantilevered Chinese fishing nets operating on wooden counterweights, St. Francis Church (where explorer Vasco da Gama was originally buried in 1524), Dutch colonial mansions transformed into boutique spice heritage hotels, and aromatic warehouses lining Jew Town.

Top Places to Visit in Kochi

1. Chinese Fishing Nets (Cheena Vala)

Introduced in the 14th century by Chinese explorer Zheng He's court, these magnificent cantilevered fishing installations line the Fort Kochi shoreline. Supported by teakwood beams and suspended stones, each net requires a coordinated team of six fishermen to lower and raise into the tidal waters.

2. Mattancherry Palace (Dutch Palace) Closed on Fridays

Constructed by the Portuguese around 1555 and later renovated by the Dutch. The palace features traditional Kerala wooden architecture with gabled roofs, housing some of India's finest 16th-century tempera murals depicting scenes from the Ramayana and Bhagavata Purana.

3. Paradesi Synagogue & Jew Town

Built in 1568 in the historic Jewish settlement of Mattancherry. The synagogue features hundreds of hand-painted 18th-century blue-and-white willow pattern porcelain floor tiles from Canton, China, brass chandeliers from Belgium, and ancient copper plates granted by the King of Cochin.

4. St. Francis Church & Santa Cruz Basilica

St. Francis Church, founded in 1503, is the oldest European church built in India. The nearby Santa Cruz Cathedral Basilica features towering Gothic spires and pastel-hued ceiling frescoes.

5. Kerala Kathakali Center

Attend an evening performance of classical Kathakali dance-drama. Arrive an hour early to witness the elaborate ritual makeup process where actors apply natural mineral pigments and elaborate paper chutti beard frames to personify gods and demons.

Suggested 2-Day Kochi Itinerary

Day 1: Fort Kochi Heritage Walk, Fishing Nets & Kathakali

Explore the tree-lined streets of Fort Kochi on foot. Photograph the Chinese Fishing Nets in operation along the beach promenade. Visit St. Francis Church and browse art cafes. In the evening, watch a classical Kathakali performance followed by dinner at a colonial courtyard restaurant.

Day 2: Mattancherry Palace, Jew Town & Harbour Ferry

Take an auto-rickshaw to Mattancherry to view the intricate mythological murals at the Dutch Palace. Browse spice shops, antique stores, and the Paradesi Synagogue in Jew Town. In the late afternoon, take the scenic public Ro-Ro ferry across the harbor to Vypeen Island before continuing your journey toward Munnar or Alleppey backwaters.

How to Reach Kochi

By Air

Cochin International Airport (COK) at Nedumbassery is 35 km northeast of Fort Kochi. Notably, it is the world's first fully solar-powered airport, serving extensive domestic routes and international hubs across the Gulf and Southeast Asia.

By Train

Two major railway stations serve the city on the mainland in Ernakulam: Ernakulam Junction (South - ERS) and Ernakulam Town (North - ERN), connected by Vande Bharat Express and superfast trains across India.

Water Metro

Kochi features India's first integrated electric Water Metro system, connecting islands and suburban hubs via clean air-conditioned catamarans.
