Srinagar Travel Guide: Dal Lake, Mughal Gardens & Kashmir Valley

Traditional cushioned Shikara gliding along the mirror-like waters of Dal Lake against the snow-dusted Zabarwan mountains.
Region Kashmir Valley, J&K
Best Time to Visit April to October (Winter for Snow)
Ideal Trip Duration 4 to 5 Days
Airport Sheikh ul-Alam Airport (SXR)
Nearest Major Railhead Jammu Tawi (JAT) / Udhampur
Local Drink Kashmiri Kahwa with Almonds

Why Visit Srinagar?

Cradled at an altitude of 1,585 meters in the heart of the Kashmir Valley, Srinagar has captivated poets and travelers for centuries as the summer jewel of northern India. The city is defined by the winding waters of the Jhelum River, five interconnected lakes, and the snow-capped amphitheater of the Pir Panjal and Zabarwan ranges.

Staying in a hand-carved cedarwood houseboat on Dal or Nigeen Lake, gliding silently on a cushioned Shikara through water lily channels, admiring terraced Persian fountains in Mughal pleasure gardens, and sipping steaming saffron Kahwa while snow falls on surrounding pines constitute an iconic Indian travel experience.

Top Places to Visit in Srinagar

1. Dal Lake & Floating Vegetable Market

Covering 18 square kilometers, Dal Lake is an intricate wetland ecosystem featuring floating gardens (radh), houseboats, and island parks (Char Chinar). At dawn (5:00 AM), experience the centuries-old Floating Vegetable Market where farmers barter fresh lotus roots (nadru), turnips, and greens on wooden skiffs.

2. Shalimar Bagh & Nishat Bagh (Mughal Gardens)

Built by Mughal Emperor Jahangir for Empress Nur Jahan in 1619, Shalimar Bagh features 12 cascading stone terraces fed by mountain springs. Nearby Nishat Bagh (Garden of Bliss) descends in dramatic terraces directly to Dal Lake, offering magnificent sunset reflections.

3. Shankaracharya Temple

Dedicated to Lord Shiva and perched atop Gopadari Hill 300 meters above the valley floor. Believed to have been visited by philosopher Adi Shankaracharya in the 9th century, its stone terrace affords an incomparable aerial view of Srinagar city and Dal Lake.

4. Indira Gandhi Memorial Tulip Garden Open Mid-March to Mid-April

Asia's largest tulip sanctuary, sprawling across 30 hectares in the foothills of the Zabarwan range. In early spring, over 1.5 million blooming tulips in 60 distinct color varieties carpet the slopes in a breathtaking floral display.

5. Day Excursions: Gulmarg & Pahalgam

Srinagar serves as the gateway for scenic valley day excursions. Gulmarg (50 km away) features the world's second-highest cable car (Gulmarg Gondola ascending to 3,980m) and winter ski slopes. Pahalgam (90 km away) offers pine-forested walks along the Lidder River and Betaab Valley.

Suggested 4-Day Srinagar Itinerary

Day 1: Arrival, Houseboat Check-in & Sunset Shikara

Arrive at Sheikh ul-Alam Airport. Check into a heritage cedarwood houseboat on Nigeen or Dal Lake. In the late afternoon, take a relaxed 2-hour Shikara ride exploring the floating lotus gardens and Char Chinar island.

Day 2: Mughal Gardens, Old City & Hazratbal Shrine

Tour the cascading fountains of Shalimar Bagh and Nishat Bagh. Visit the serene white marble dome of Hazratbal Shrine on the northern shore of Dal Lake. Explore the Jamia Masjid in the Old City with its 378 deodar wood pillars.

Day 3: Day Trip to Gulmarg & Gondola Ride

Drive 2 hours to Gulmarg. Ride Phase 1 and Phase 2 of the Gulmarg Gondola up to Apharwat Peak for panoramic views of Nanga Parbat. Enjoy snow sledding or meadow walks before returning to Srinagar for dinner.

Day 4: Dawn Floating Market, Saffron & Pashmina Shopping

Experience the 5:30 AM sunrise Floating Market on Dal Lake. Visit government-certified artisan showrooms for pure hand-spun Pashmina shawls, walnut wood furniture, and Kashmiri saffron before your return flight.

How to Reach Srinagar

By Air

Sheikh ul-Alam International Airport (SXR) is situated 12 km from Dal Lake Boulevard, offering frequent direct flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Chandigarh, Bengaluru, and Amritsar.

By Train & Road

The Udhampur-Srinagar-Baramulla Rail Link (USBRL) connecting Kashmir to the national broad-gauge railway network via the Chenab Railway Bridge makes train access increasingly direct. Road connectivity from Jammu operates via NH 44 through the 8.5 km Navayuga Banihal Qazigund Tunnel.
