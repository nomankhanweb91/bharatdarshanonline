Jaipur Travel Guide: Royal Forts, Palaces & Pink City Itinerary

Why Visit Jaipur?

Founded in 1727 by the visionary astronomer-king Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II, Jaipur was India's first planned city built on ancient Vastu Shastra principles. In 1876, the entire walled city was coated in warm terracotta pink—the traditional color of hospitality—to welcome the Prince of Wales, earning its enduring moniker: the Pink City.

Jaipur offers an irresistible encounter with Rajput valor and Mughal-era finesse: clifftop citadels gazing over rugged Aravalli ridges, opulent palace courtyards with shimmering mirrored ceilings, the world's largest stone sundial, and vibrant bazaars brimming with block-printed textiles, blue pottery, and handcrafted gemstone jewelry.

Top Places to Visit in Jaipur

1. Amer Fort (Amber Palace) Open: 8:00 AM – 5:30 PM & Night Tourism

A breathtaking UNESCO World Heritage fortress perched high on the rugged hills above Maota Lake. Explore the breathtaking Sheesh Mahal (Mirror Palace) where candlelight mirrors recreate a starry galaxy, the Diwan-i-Aam, and the Sukh Niwas cooling gardens.

2. Hawa Mahal (Palace of Winds) Open: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM

Built in 1799 by Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh, this five-story pyramid-like façade features 953 intricately carved jharokhas (latticed windows). Its ingenious airflow design allowed royal women to observe bustling street festivities along Badi Chaupar without being observed from outside.

3. City Palace & Chandra Mahal Heart of the Walled City

A sprawling royal residence combining Rajput, Mughal, and European architectural elements. Admire the Peacock Gate in Pritam Niwas Chowk, tour the royal textile and armory museum, and observe the monumental silver vessels (Gangajali) crafted for Maharaja Madho Singh II's voyage to England.

4. Jantar Mantar Observatory UNESCO World Heritage Site

The world's most sophisticated collection of 19 architectural astronomical instruments made of local stone and marble, featuring the Samrat Yantra—the world's largest stone sundial measuring time accurate to within two seconds.

5. Nahargarh Fort & Jaigarh Fort Best: 4:30 PM – Sunset

Perched along the ridge of the Aravalli Hills, Nahargarh provides the definitive panoramic sunset view over the entire expanse of Jaipur city. Nearby Jaigarh Fort houses the legendary Jaivana cannon—once the largest cannon on wheels in the world.

Suggested 3-Day Jaipur Itinerary

Day 1: City Palace, Jantar Mantar, Hawa Mahal & Old Bazaars

Start in the historic walled city exploring the royal courtyards of the City Palace and the scientific marvel of Jantar Mantar. Photograph the facade of Hawa Mahal from the street-level cafes. Spend the evening browsing silver jewelry and textiles in Johari Bazar and Bapu Bazar.

Day 2: Amer Fort, Jaigarh & Sunset at Nahargarh

Head 11 km north to Amer Fort in the morning before crowds arrive. Ascend through the Sun Gate to tour the Mirror Palace. Walk through the underground secret tunnel connecting to Jaigarh Fort. End the day sipping tea as the sunset lights illuminate the Pink City from the ramparts of Nahargarh Fort.

Day 3: Albert Hall Museum, Jal Mahal & Craft Villages

Admire Indo-Saracenic architecture at the Albert Hall Museum. Stop by the lakefront promenade to admire Jal Mahal (Water Palace). Visit Sanganer to witness traditional hand-block printing workshops and blue pottery artisans before departure.

How to Reach Jaipur

By Air

Jaipur International Airport (JAI) at Sanganer connects directly with Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, and international hubs in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and Sharjah.

By Train

Jaipur Junction (JP) is linked with daily Vande Bharat Express and Ajmer Shatabdi trains from New Delhi (under 4 hours), as well as direct superfast services to Mumbai, Agra, and Ahmedabad.

By Road

The Delhi-Mumbai Expressway and NH 48 link Delhi to Jaipur in approximately 3.5 to 4.5 hours. Excellent state highway networks connect onward to Udaipur, Jodhpur, and Bikaner.
