Delhi Travel Guide: Historic Monuments, Culture & Itinerary

Why Visit Delhi?

Delhi is one of the world's most historically saturated capitals. Having served as the seat of power for Tomar Rajputs, the Delhi Sultanate, the Mughal Dynasty, and the British Empire, the city seamlessly encapsulates eight distinct historical settlements within one metropolis.

Here, visitors witness the juxtaposition of Old Delhi's labyrinthine 17th-century walled city (Shahjahanabad)—perfumed with the spices of Khari Baoli—alongside the stately tree-lined boulevards and neoclassical government complexes designed by Edwin Lutyens. It is home to three UNESCO World Heritage Sites: Qutub Minar, Humayun's Tomb, and the Red Fort.

Top Places to Visit in Delhi

1. Qutub Minar Complex UNESCO World Heritage Site

The world's tallest brick minaret at 72.5 meters, founded in 1192 by Qutb-ud-din Aibak to mark the dawn of the Delhi Sultanate. Within the complex stands the enigmatic 4th-century Gupta-era Iron Pillar of Chandragupta II, which has resisted rust and corrosion for over 1,600 years.

2. Humayun's Tomb UNESCO World Heritage Site

Commissioned in 1569 by Empress Bega Begum, this red sandstone and white marble charbagh garden tomb introduced Persian geometric elegance to Mughal architecture, providing the direct architectural inspiration for the Taj Mahal decades later.

3. The Red Fort (Lal Qila) & Jama Masjid Closed on Mondays

Constructed in 1639 by Emperor Shah Jahan as the administrative fortress of Shahjahanabad. Marvel at the Lahore Gate, Diwan-i-Am, and the exquisite marble pavilions of Diwan-i-Khas. Just opposite lies Jama Masjid, one of India's largest mosques.

4. India Gate & Kartavya Path

A 42-meter triumphal triumphal arch memorializing 84,000 soldiers of the British Indian Army who lost their lives in World War I. Stroll along the renovated Kartavya Path to view Rashtrapati Bhavan (Presidential Palace) illuminated at dusk.

5. Lotus Temple & Akshardham Complex

The Lotus Temple (Bahá'í House of Worship) features 27 free-standing petals made of pure Greek marble, inviting visitors of all faiths for silent meditation. The monumental Swaminarayan Akshardham displays traditional Indian stone-carving traditions, boat rides, and evening musical fountain shows.

Suggested 3-Day Delhi Itinerary

Day 1: Old Delhi Heritage, Jama Masjid & Chandni Chowk

Take the Delhi Metro (Yellow Line) to Chandni Chowk. Tour the red sandstone ramparts of the Red Fort and the vast courtyard of Jama Masjid. Take a cycle rickshaw ride through Dariba Kalan and Paranthe Wali Gali, sampling parathas and jalebis.

Day 2: Qutub Minar, Mehrauli & Humayun's Tomb

Explore South Delhi's heritage beginning at Qutub Minar. Walk through the tranquil garden courtyards of Humayun's Tomb and Isa Khan's tomb. In the afternoon, explore the craft stalls of Dilli Haat (INA) for authentic regional handicraft shopping and state food pavilions.

Day 3: Lutyens' Delhi, National Museum & Akshardham

Stroll past India Gate and the National War Memorial. Tour the ancient sculptures (including the Harappan Dancing Girl) at the National Museum. In the evening, cross the Yamuna River to witness the sound, light, and water show at Akshardham Temple.

How to Reach Delhi

By Air

Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL) is India's busiest aviation gateway, operating three terminals and connected to central Delhi in 20 minutes via the super-fast Airport Express Metro.

By Train

Connected via four mega terminals: New Delhi (NDLS), Hazrat Nizamuddin (NZM), Old Delhi (DLI), and Anand Vihar (ANVT), linking every corner of the nation.

Urban Transit

The Delhi Metro spans over 390 km across 12 lines, providing clean, air-conditioned, reliable transportation to nearly all major tourist monuments.
