Amritsar Travel Guide: The Golden Temple, Heritage & Wagah Border

Why Visit Amritsar?

Founded in 1577 by the fourth Sikh Guru, Guru Ram Das, Amritsar (literally meaning "Pool of the Nectar of Immortality") is the preeminent spiritual and cultural sanctuary of Sikhism. Located in northwestern Punjab near the international border, the city emanates profound warmth, hospitality, and selfless community service (Seva).

The radiant Golden Temple welcomes devotees of all faiths through its four open entrances, symbolizing universal equality. Beyond spiritual solace, Amritsar holds solemn national significance at the Jallianwala Bagh memorial, electric patriotic fervor at the Wagah Border ceremony, and unmatched culinary renown for buttery tandoori kulchas, dal makhani, and rich churned lassi.

Top Places to Visit in Amritsar

1. Sri Harmandir Sahib (The Golden Temple) Open 24 Hours Daily

Gilded with over 500 kilograms of pure gold foil during Maharaja Ranjit Singh's reign, the two-story sanctum stands in the middle of the sacred Amrit Sarovar. Devotees walk across the marble Guru's Bridge causeway while continuous Gurbani kirtan resonates across the parikrama.

Guru Ka Langar: The world's largest community kitchen serves hot, nutritious, vegetarian meals to over 100,000 visitors every single day free of cost, run entirely by dedicated community volunteers.

2. Jallianwala Bagh Memorial Short walk from Golden Temple

The sacred national memorial preserving the site of the April 13, 1919 massacre where General Dyer ordered troops to fire upon peaceful Baisakhi gatherers. The site features the Martyrs' Well, the Eternal Flame, and bullet marks preserved in the brick perimeter walls.

3. Wagah Border Beating Retreat Ceremony 30 km from City Center

Held every evening before sunset at the Indo-Pak border gate. Indian Border Security Force (BSF) soldiers and Pakistan Rangers perform an energetic, theatrical military drill featuring high kicks, coordinated stomps, and formal flag-lowering accompanied by cheering crowds.

4. Partition Museum (Town Hall) Closed on Mondays

The world's first museum dedicated to the 1947 Partition of India. Housed in the restored colonial Town Hall, it features audio recordings, letters, family artifacts, and historical documentation depicting the largest mass migration in human history.

5. Gobindgarh Fort & Heritage Street

An 18th-century military fort built by Gujjar Singh Bhangi and fortified by Maharaja Ranjit Singh, featuring the Toshakhana treasury, war museums, and live Punjabi martial arts (Gatka) performances.

Suggested 2-Day Amritsar Itinerary

Day 1: Golden Temple Parikrama, Langar & Evening Illumination

Cover your head and enter through the marble arches of the Golden Temple at sunrise. Partake in the blessed community Guru ka Langar. Visit the poignant Jallianwala Bagh memorial and the Partition Museum. In the evening, witness the divine Palki Sahib ceremony as the holy Guru Granth Sahib is carried in a flower-bedecked palanquin to the Akal Takht for the night.

Day 2: Heritage Walk, Street Food & Wagah Border

Stroll through the pedestrianized Heritage Street. Enjoy breakfast of crispy stuffed potato and paneer Amritsari Kulcha with chole at Kulcha Land. Depart by 2:30 PM for the Wagah Border to secure stadium seats for the 4:30 PM Beating Retreat ceremony. Return to Old Amritsar for shopping of Phulkari dupattas and Punjabi juttis.

How to Reach Amritsar

By Air

Sri Guru Ram Dass Jee International Airport (ATQ) lies 11 km northwest of the city, offering direct domestic flights to Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, and international flights to London, Birmingham, Dubai, and Singapore.

By Train

Amritsar Junction (ASR) is served by the high-speed Vande Bharat Express and Shatabdi Express from New Delhi (taking approximately 5 to 6 hours), alongside express connections across North and Central India.

By Road

National Highway 44 (Grand Trunk Road) links Delhi to Amritsar (450 km) via a multi-lane expressway passing Panipat, Ambala, Ludhiana, and Jalandhar.
